> **One-line summary:** Monash University and Yale researchers are developing non-invasive devices to assist brain-draining lymphatic vessels, promoting the clearance of toxic waste to treat ischemic stroke and neurodegenerative conditions.
>
> **Key points:**
> - Researchers are targeting "brain-draining lymphatics" in the neck to improve the clearance of toxic fluids and waste following an ischemic stroke.
> - The team is developing non-invasive medical devices designed to enhance the pumping efficiency of these lymphatic vessels during critical post-stroke windows.
> - Impairment of this waste-clearing network is closely linked to Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders.
> - Early imaging highlights physiological differences between male and female brain-draining lymphatics, paving the way for gender-tailored neurological therapies.
> - The online discussion also explored the potential therapeutic benefits of N-acetylcysteine (NAC) and lymphatic massage in improving cerebral lymphatic drainage.
