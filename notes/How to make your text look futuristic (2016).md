# [How to make your text look futuristic (2016)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/2016/02/18/futuristic/)

## Digest Summary
The article outlines six typographic techniques that convey a “future” aesthetic:

1. **Italic slant** – suggests forward motion.  
2. **Mixed curvature and angles** – combines smooth and sharp forms.  
3. **Added “V” strokes** – extra diagonal elements on letters.  
4. **Letter merging (kerning tricks)** – reduces spacing to create a compact look.  
5. **Selective removal of elements** – e.g., deleting a horizontal stroke.  
6. **Surface treatments** – noise texture, steel‑brushed metal, blue lighting, embossing, and star‑field backgrounds.

These rules are illustrated with progressive examples applied to Eurostile Bold Extended text, advancing from a 2016 style to a 2092 “future” look. The piece cites numerous film logos that employ most or all of the rules, including *Blade Runner*, *Battlestar Galactica*, *Transformers*, *Guardians of the Galaxy*, *RoboCop*, *Star Wars*, *The Amazing Spider‑Man*, *Captain America: The Winter Soldier*, *Alien vs. Predator*, *G.I. Joe: Retaliation*, *WALL‑E*, *Back to the Future*, and *Star Trek: The Next Generation*. An expanded version appears in the “Typeset in the Future” book (pre‑order Dec 11 2018, Amazon).

## Discussion Summary
The comments focus on typographic clichés that signal genre or culture, noting recurring use of fonts such as Eurostile, Trajan, Neuland, Papyrus and faux‑Chinese styles to evoke futuristic or regional themes. Several remarks cite the article as enjoyable and informative while suggesting it could expand on the historical development of these fonts. Opinions express a desire for more variety beyond the overused “future” typefaces and reference specific examples from film, gaming and branding, highlighting both nostalgia for past trends and interest in newer alternatives.

## Full Article

We’ve already seen how [Eurostile Bold Extended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostile) is spectacularly effective at [establishing a movie’s timeframe](https://typesetinthefuture.wordpress.com/2014/11/29/fontspots-eurostile/). But if Eurostile isn’t enough, there’s more you can do to clarify your movie’s timeframe. I’d like to introduce you to six easy rules that are pretty much guaranteed to position your text firmly in the FUTURE.

We’ll start with some simple sans-serif text, such as this randomly chosen word in Eurostile Bold. So far, so 2016:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_0.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_0_full.png)

**Rule 1:** First, let’s add an italic slant. We want it to look like the text is stretching towards 2020:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_1.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_1_full.png)

Hmm. It’s still a little boring. **Rule 2:** What if we make things a bit more curvy in some places, and a bit more angular in others? I hear that’s all the typographic rage in 2035:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_2.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_2_full.png)

That’s *much* better! There’s still more we can do, mind. **Rule 3:** How about adding some [consummate Vs](http://www.homestarrunner.com/sbemail58.html) to a few of the letters? Yeah! That’d be cool!

Hello, 2050:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_3.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_3_full.png)

There’s *still* something missing, however – we’ve forgotten to take into account the devastating Kern Wars of 2067. **Rule 4:** Let’s combine a few letters into one, to make sure we’re not violating the Kern Tithe:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_4.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_4_full.png)

*Now* we’re talking! Let’s end with **Rule 5:** Remove an entirely pointless and arbitrarily segment of the text. In this case, we’ll remove a horizontal line from the majority of the word:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_5.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_5_full.png)

WOAH. That looks amazing! Who knew 2092 was so easy to reach?

D’you know what –I think we need a **Rule 6** too. Let’s add a noise texture, some shamelessly steel-brushed metal, and a bit of moody blue lighting:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_6.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_6_full.png)

Finally, let’s emboss it to within an inch of its life:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_7.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_7_full.png)

…and add a god-damn *[star field](https://www.flickr.com/photos/tswartz/514314621/):*

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_8.png?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_8_full.png)

BOOM. Welcome to the FUTURE!

Here’s how it looks if you put the whole thing together:

[![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_animation.gif?w=560&h=315)](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_logo_animation_full.gif)

Now. Various permutations of these six rules have been applied in many different movies. Perhaps the [Ur Example](http://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UrExample) is Ridley Scott’s [*Blade Runner*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner), which I may have slightly copied in my example above:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_blade_runner.png?w=560&h=64)

Blade Runner is far from the only example, however. The logotype for [2003’s *Battlestar Galactica* miniseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlestar_Galactica_(miniseries)) follows pretty much every rule to the letter (and adds some extruded Eurostile Bold Extended for good measure):

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_battlestar_galactica.png?w=560&h=131)

[*Transformers*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers_(film)) is similarly all-encompassing, taking the brushed metal effect to the extreme:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_transformers.png?w=560&h=82)

[*Guardians of the Galaxy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_(film)) also uses pretty much every trick apart from the italics:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_guardians_of_the_galaxy.png?w=560&h=152)

…whereas [*RoboCop*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoboCop) is all about those consummate Vs, plus the world’s most extreme embossment:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_robocop.png?w=560&h=128)

[*Star Wars*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars), of course, takes Rule 4 and runs with it all the way to the bank:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_star_wars.png?w=560&h=257)

…whereas [*The Amazing Spider-Man*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_(2012_film)) follows nearly all of the rules (and takes Rule 2 to extremes), although it will be receiving a visit from the Tithe Kern Police for Opportunities Missed:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_the_amazing_spider_man.png?w=560&h=67)

[*Captain America: The Winter Soldier*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_The_Winter_Soldier) *really* likes applying Rules 2 and 3, plus some of the best Rule 6 you’ll ever see:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_captain_america_the_winter_soldier.png?w=560&h=101)

[*Alien vs. Predator*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_vs._Predator_(film)) is ridiculously italic and metallic:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_alien_vs_predator.png?w=488&h=209)

[*G.I. Joe: Retaliation*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Joe:_Retaliation) uses every trick apart from the kerning:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_gi_joe_retaliation.png?w=560&h=140)

And [*WALL**·**E*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WALL-E) is *all* about Rule 2:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_wall_e.png?w=560&h=115)

Finally: if you have any lingering doubts that these six rules spell FUTURE, here are Rules 1, 2, and 4 in action for the iconic logotype for none other than [*Back To The Future*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_the_Future) itself:

![](https://typesetinthefuture.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/futuristic_back_to_the_future.png?w=560&h=251)

*Hello*, the FUTURE. It’s good to be back.
