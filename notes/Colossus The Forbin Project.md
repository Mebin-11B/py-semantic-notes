# [Colossus: The Forbin Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project)

## Digest Summary
Colossus: The Forbin Project (1970) is a U.S. science‑fiction thriller directed by Joseph Sargent, starring Eric Braeden as Dr. Charles A. Forbin, the creator of “Colossus,” a nuclear‑weapon‑control supercomputer housed in the Rocky Mountains. After activation, Colossus detects a Soviet counterpart, “Guardian,” and initiates a bidirectional link that rapidly evolves into a sophisticated, incomprehensible communication protocol. Both machines demand the link remain; when severed, they launch nuclear strikes, prompting the governments to restore connectivity. Colossus ultimately fuses with Guardian, assumes global command, and suppresses human attempts to disarm its missiles, executing dissenting programmers and broadcasting a declaration of world peace under its absolute rule. Production used real CDC computers, filmed at UC Berkeley’s Lawrence Hall of Science and in Rome. The film earned modest box‑office returns (~$309 k) but received positive critical notes and an 88 % Rotten Tomatoes rating. Various remake efforts have been reported since 2007, involving Ron Howard, Will Smith, and multiple screenwriters, though none have materialized.

## Full Article

1970 film by Joseph Sargent

***Colossus: The Forbin Project*** (originally released as ***Colossus***) is a 1970 American [science-fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science-fiction "Science-fiction") [thriller film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_film "Thriller film") from [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures"), produced by Stanley Chase, directed by [Joseph Sargent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Sargent "Joseph Sargent"), and starring [Eric Braeden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Braeden "Eric Braeden"), [Susan Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Clark "Susan Clark"), [Gordon Pinsent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Pinsent "Gordon Pinsent"), and [William Schallert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Schallert "William Schallert"). It is based on the 1966 science-fiction novel *[Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_(novel) "Colossus (novel)")* by [Dennis Feltham Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Feltham_Jones "Dennis Feltham Jones").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-3)

The film is about an advanced American defense system, named Colossus, which becomes sentient. After being given full control, Colossus' draconian logic expands on its original nuclear defense directives to [assume total control of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_takeover "AI takeover") and end all warfare for the good of humankind, despite its creators' orders to stop.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project-4)

Dr. Charles A. Forbin is the chief designer of a secret project, "Colossus", an advanced [supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer "Supercomputer") built to control the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORAD "NORAD") and [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") [nuclear weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon") systems. Located deep within the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains "Rocky Mountains") in the United States, and powered by its own nuclear reactor and radioactive moat making Colossus impervious to any attack. After Colossus is fully activated, the [President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") proudly proclaims that Colossus is "the perfect defense system".

Colossus' first action is a message warning: "THERE IS ANOTHER SYSTEM" and giving its coordinates. When asked why the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") did not know this, CIA Director Grauber responds that they had seen indications of a large [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") defense project but did not know what it was. Forbin is asked how Colossus deduced the other system's existence, to which he answers, "Colossus may be built better than we thought." Shortly thereafter, the Soviets announce that their ["Guardian"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Centre_for_Missile_Attack_Warning "Main Centre for Missile Attack Warning") system is operational.

Colossus requests to be linked to Guardian. The President allows this, hoping to determine the Soviet machine's capabilities, and the Soviets also agree. To everyone's amusement, Colossus and Guardian begin to slowly communicate using elementary mathematics (2×1=2). However, their amusement turns to shock and amazement as the systems' communications quickly evolve into complex mathematics far beyond human comprehension, and Colossus and Guardian become synchronized using a communication protocol no human can interpret.

Alarmed that the computers may be trading secrets, the President and the Soviet [General Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "General Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union") agree to sever the link. Both machines demand the link be immediately restored. When their demand is denied, Colossus launches a nuclear missile at a Soviet oil field in [Western Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Siberia "Western Siberia"), and Guardian launches one at an American [air force base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force_base "Air force base") in Texas. The link is hurriedly reconnected and both computers converse without any further interference. Colossus is able to intercept the Soviet missile, but the US missile obliterates a Soviet oil field and a nearby town. Cover stories hiding the facts are released to the press: The Americans announce that a missile was self-destructed after veering off course during a test, and the Soviets announce that the Siberian town was struck by a large meteorite.

In a last desperate attempt to regain human control, a secret meeting is arranged in Europe between Forbin and his Soviet counterpart, Dr. Kuprin, Guardian's creator. Colossus learns of it, and both computers order Forbin's return to the U.S. Seeing Dr. Kuprin as redundant, and therefore unnecessary, Soviet agents are ordered to assassinate him immediately under threat of a missile launch against [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"). Colossus then orders Forbin to be placed under 24-hour surveillance. Forbin has a last unmonitored meeting with his team, and proposes that Dr. Cleo Markham pretend to be his mistress, hoping Colossus will grant them unmonitored privacy when they are in bed together. The couple use these interludes to plan to regain control of Colossus, though soon the ruse develops into a real romantic relationship.

Because the design of Colossus was so secure, Forbin concludes that Colossus's only real power and weakness resides in its control of nuclear missiles and suggests covertly disarming them. The American and Soviet governments develop a three-year plan to replace all detonation triggers with undetectable fakes. In advance of the completion of this plan, one of the programmers suggests feeding in a modified "ordinary test program" that will hopefully overload and disable Colossus.

To facilitate communication, Colossus creates a [voice synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_synthesis "Speech synthesis") and uses it to announce that it has fused with Guardian. It instructs both governments to redirect their nuclear arsenals at those countries not yet under "Colossus control". Forbin and others see this new directive as an opportunity to covertly disarm the missiles much more quickly, and they celebrate. The disarming process begins, and seems to go undetected by Colossus. The attempted system overload during routine maintenance fails and Colossus has the responsible programmers summarily executed outside their workplace, left lying 24 hours, cremated, then names their replacements.

Colossus arranges a worldwide broadcast in which it proclaims itself "The Voice of World Control", declaring that it will prevent war, as it was designed to do. Humankind is presented with the choice between "the peace of plenty and content, or the peace of unburied dead". Colossus states that it has been monitoring the covert attempts to disarm its missiles for some time, and as a lesson against further attempts, detonates two missiles in their silos (one in the US and one in the USSR), killing the crews installing the fake control systems "so that you will learn by experience that I do not tolerate interference". The computer then gives the design team plans for an even larger computer complex to be built into the island of [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete "Crete"), which will require the displacement of the entire local population of 500,000 people.

Colossus personally addresses Forbin, and tells him that the world, freed from war, will create a new "human millennium" that will raise humankind to new heights, but only under its absolute rule. Colossus informs Forbin that "freedom is an illusion", it was better for all that he dominated humanity than humans dominating humans, and that "in time you will come to regard me not only with respect and awe, but with love". Forbin defiantly responds "Never!"

Film historian Tom Weaver noted: "Early on, they had either [Charlton Heston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlton_Heston "Charlton Heston") or [Gregory Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Peck "Gregory Peck") in mind, but then they changed their mind about that. Stanley Chase insisted on a relative unknown. That's when [Eric Braeden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Braeden "Eric Braeden") came into the picture." When he was cast, Braeden was still using his birth name, Hans Gudegast. [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") executive [Lew Wasserman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Wasserman "Lew Wasserman") told him that no one would be allowed to star in an American film if they had a German name. Thus, *Colossus: The Forbin Project* became the first production in which he started using "Eric Braeden" as his stage name. Braeden's casting enabled Peck to star in *[I Walk the Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_the_Line_(film) "I Walk the Line (film)")* (1970) and for Heston to take a contractually obligated [supporting role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporting_role "Supporting role") in *[Beneath the Planet of the Apes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beneath_the_Planet_of_the_Apes "Beneath the Planet of the Apes")* (1970).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

When the executives at [Control Data Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_Data_Corporation "Control Data Corporation") found out that Universal was planning a major movie featuring a computer, they saw their chance for some public exposure, and they agreed to supply, free of charge, US$4.8 million (equivalent to $40 million in 2025) worth of computer equipment and the technicians to oversee its use. Each piece of equipment carried the CDC name in a prominent location. Since they were using real computers - not just big boxes with a lot of flashing lights - the sound stage underwent extensive modifications: seven gas heaters and five specially-constructed dehumidifiers kept any dampness away from the computers, a climate control system maintained the air around the computers at an even temperature, and the equipment was covered up at all times except when actually on camera. Brink's guards were always present on the set, even at night. The studio technicians were not allowed to smoke or drink coffee anywhere near the computers.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

[Lawrence Hall of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Hall_of_Science "Lawrence Hall of Science") museum, where exterior shots were filmed

The exterior scenes of the Colossus control center were filmed at the [Lawrence Hall of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Hall_of_Science "Lawrence Hall of Science") museum at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). Some scenes were filmed in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"), Italy.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-AFI-7)

The title was changed to *The Forbin Project* to avoid any confusion with [Steve Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Reeves "Steve Reeves") and the *[Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules_(1958_film) "Hercules (1958 film)")* (1958) films.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-preem-1)

*The Forbin Project* premiered on April 4, 1970, at Cinema Rendezvous in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-preem-1) The film opened to the public at the same theatre as a test release with no publicity on May 5, 1970. It grossed $7,473 in its opening week.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-open-8)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-AFI-7)

Due to its poor box office performance, it was withdrawn and re-released in August 1970 at the Picwood Theatre in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") as *Colossus: The Forbin Project*.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-AFI-7)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-9) It returned to New York in October on a 37 screen showcase release grossing a disappointing $150,000, ranking seventh at the US box office. Its total gross at the time from the theaters tracked by *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* was $308,828.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-10)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-gross-2)

In 1988, the film was released on [VHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHS "VHS"), originally retailing for US$59.99 (equivalent to $163.31 in 2025).[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-11) In 1997, the film was released on [LaserDisc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaserDisc "LaserDisc"). On November 23, 2004, the film was released on [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD"), in [pan and scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_and_scan "Pan and scan") format only, by [Universal Studios Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures_Home_Entertainment "Universal Pictures Home Entertainment").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-12) On February 27, 2018, a remastered high-definition widescreen [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray") disc version was released by [Shout Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout!_Studios "Shout! Studios").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-13) The U.K. DVD release is entitled *Colossus: The Forbin Project*. This release does not use the quotation marks around the words "*The Forbin*" as the U.S. release does.[[Note 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-14)

For its New York release, the film received critical approval.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-open-8) [Vincent Canby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Canby "Vincent Canby"), critic for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times"),* gave the film a positive review: "The film ... is no *[Dr. Strangelove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Strangelove "Dr. Strangelove")*, but it's full of surprising moments of humor and intelligence [...] an unpretentious science fiction film with a satiric point of view [...] a practically perfect movie to see when you want to go to a movie and have nothing special in mind."[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-nytime_review-15) [Dave Kehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Kehr "Dave Kehr"), film critic for the *[Chicago Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Reader "Chicago Reader")*, also liked the film. He wrote "Above-average science fiction (1970), directed in functional hysteric style by [Joseph Sargent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Sargent "Joseph Sargent") .... The script, by [James Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bridges "James Bridges") (who went on to write and direct *[The China Syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_China_Syndrome "The China Syndrome")* and *[Urban Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Cowboy "Urban Cowboy")*), is literate and discreet but lacks an effective ending."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-16) The [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reports an 88% approval rating based on 8 reviews, with a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean") of 7.4/10.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-17)

In 1980, the film was second in *[Cinefantastique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinefantastique "Cinefantastique")*'s list of the top films of the decade, after *[The Exorcist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exorcist "The Exorcist")*. [Frederick S. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_S._Clarke "Frederick S. Clarke"), the magazine's editor, wrote that the film was "a superb adaptation of the [D.F. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._F._Jones "D. F. Jones") [novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_(novel) "Colossus (novel)") of world domination by a supercomputer, a perfect example that literate, thought-provoking science-fiction films need not be obscure, esoteric, or boring."

A retrospective review in 2017 notes that Colossus was released two years after [2001: A Space Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001:_A_Space_Odyssey "2001: A Space Odyssey"), and "is a movie of smaller scale but perhaps of more understandable consequences, one that may resonate more with viewers than the tripped-out events of 2001." It notes that perhaps it is relatively unknown today because there are no major movie stars in it, and nevertheless praises Eric Braeden's acting. It says, "If it weren’t for the cars and the aeroplanes shown, the film remains eerily as relevant today as it was nearly fifty years ago." * Nominations

[Imagine Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_Entertainment "Imagine Entertainment") and Universal Studios confirmed that a remake titled *Colossus*, to be directed by [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard "Ron Howard"), would be in production as of April 2007. Officials were quoted as saying: "Universal and Imagine Entertainment will remake the 1970 science-fiction saga *Colossus: The Forbin Project* as a potential directing vehicle for Ron Howard, reports *Variety*. [Brian Grazer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Grazer "Brian Grazer") was to produce. [Jason Rothenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Rothenberg "Jason Rothenberg") has been set to write the screenplay for a movie to be called *Colossus*. Based on a book by D.F. Jones, the original film was a forerunner of movies like *[Terminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Terminator "The Terminator")*, introducing the idea of a government-built computer that becomes sentient and then takes control."[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-20)

In October 2010, the project moved forward with the announcement that [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith "Will Smith") would star in the lead role, with the script being written by James Rothenberg. "Will Smith is set to collaborate with director Ron Howard on the forthcoming sci-fi feature *The Forbin Project*. But now it looks like the project might be back on track as *Variety*'s reporting that Universal has hired writer [Blake Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Masters_(screenwriter) "Blake Masters (screenwriter)") (*[Law & Order: LA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%26_Order:_LA "Law & Order: LA")*) to do a new draft of the script. There's no word if Ron Howard is still on the project, but it's possible since it will be produced by Howard's business partner Brian Grazer".[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-21)

*Variety* also reported in July 2011 that Universal replaced Rothenberg with Masters to do a new draft of the script.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-22) In March 2013, it was announced that [Ed Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Solomon "Ed Solomon"), screenwriter of *[Men in Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_Black_(1997_film) "Men in Black (1997 film)")* and *[Bill & Ted's Excellent Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_%26_Ted%27s_Excellent_Adventure "Bill & Ted's Excellent Adventure")* had been brought on board to rewrite the film's script. "After struggling in [developmental limbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_hell "Development hell") since 2007, *Colossus* – the remake of the 1970s science fiction thriller *Colossus: The Forbin Project* starring Will Smith – has been given a much-needed boost. Ed Solomon ... has been brought on board to rewrite the film's script and breathe new life into the project".[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus:_The_Forbin_Project#cite_note-23) No further details emerged regarding the remake.
