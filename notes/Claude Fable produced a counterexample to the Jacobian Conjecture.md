# [Claude Fable produced a counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture](https://xcancel.com/__alpoge__/status/2079028340955197566)

## Digest Summary
A polynomial map \(F:\mathbb{C}^3\to\mathbb{C}^3\) is presented:
\[
F(x,y,z)=\big((1+xy)^3z+y^2(1+xy)(4+3xy),\; y+3x(1+xy)^2z+3xy^2(4+3xy),\; 2x-3x^2y-x^3z\big).
\]
The Jacobian determinant of \(F\) is constant \(-2\). The map sends the three distinct points
\((0,0,-\tfrac14)\), \((1,-\tfrac32,\tfrac{13}2)\), and \((-1,\tfrac32,\tfrac{13}2)\) to the identical image \((-\,\tfrac14,0,0)\). This provides a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture in dimension three, showing that a polynomial map with non‑zero constant Jacobian determinant need not be invertible. The author attributes the insight to collaborators but the mathematical content is limited to the explicit map, its constant Jacobian, and the three pre‑images of a single point.
